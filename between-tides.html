Between Tides explores the space between identity and transformation, the moment when something familiar begins to feel otherworldly. Set against the ocean at dusk, the story follows a woman caught between two states of being. The changing tide represents the shift in identity, in herself — someone who is constantly moving, retreating, returning, never entirely still.

Creative Direction
Between Tides

THE IDEA

I wanted to create a story about being caught somewhere in between — who you are and who you are becoming.

The ocean felt like the right place for that. There is something about being near water that makes everything feel a little less certain. Things move, disappear and come back differently.

I wanted the woman in these images to feel like she was going through that kind of shift.

The silver dress, the water, the darkness and the flashes of red light were all ways of creating a world that felt familiar but slightly unreal. I was especially interested in using movement and long exposure to make her appear almost doubled — as if we were seeing versions of her at the same time.

The story isn't really about choosing between those versions.

It's about existing somewhere between them.

VISUAL DIRECTION

I wanted the images to feel dreamlike, intimate and a little unsettling.

I was drawn to the contrast between the softness of the ocean and the harshness of the light. The silver dress catches whatever light is around it, while the darker surroundings make her feel almost like she doesn't completely belong there.

I also wanted to leave some things unresolved. Rather than making everything feel perfectly composed, I liked the moments where the movement, light or blur created something unexpected.

That became an important part of the story for me — letting the image feel like a memory rather than a perfectly captured moment.

THE STORY

ARRIVAL
The world is still familiar.

SHIFT
Movement begins to change how we see her.

BETWEEN
Her reflection, her body and her surroundings start to overlap.

BECOMING
By the end, it feels less like we're looking at the same person we met at the beginning.
