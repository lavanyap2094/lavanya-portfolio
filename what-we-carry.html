WHAT WE CARRY

An exploration of inheritance — not only of tradition, but of feeling.

There are things we inherit without ever being taught: the gestures of the women before us, the rituals woven into ordinary life, the way we hold ourselves, the memories that somehow feel older than our own.

Rooted in the quiet textures of Indian womanhood, this story explores the invisible thread between lineage and identity. The water becomes a metaphor for memory that is always moving, yet carrying traces of what came before. The lotus, held close to the body, becomes a symbol of something inherited and continually renewed.

It is a story about belonging to a place, to a lineage, and to the parts of ourselves that existed before we knew how to name them.

THE IDEA

I started thinking about the things we inherit without ever consciously choosing them.

The stories we grow up hearing.
The clothes we see our mothers and grandmothers wear.
The gestures, traditions and ways of seeing that get passed down.
The things that belong to our family long before they belong to us.

For me, What We Carry is also about lineage and the idea that we carry pieces of the people who came before us, even when we don't always realize it.

I wanted to explore that through fashion and the body. Clothing felt like a natural way into the story because it can hold so much history. A piece of fabric can carry a place, a memory, a person, or a tradition.

But lineage isn't only about preserving what came before.

We also change it.

We take what was given to us and make it our own. We keep certain things, leave others behind, and sometimes return to them years later with a completely different understanding.

What we carry becomes part of who we are, but it doesn't have to define who we become.

VISUAL DIRECTION

I wanted the images to feel connected to memory without looking like a literal recreation of the past.

I was interested in the tension between something inherited and something contemporary — familiar forms placed in a new context, traditional references alongside a more personal way of styling and seeing.

The clothing becomes more than just fashion. It becomes a connection between generations.

I wanted there to be a sense of intimacy in the photographs, almost as if we're looking at fragments of a story that existed before the camera arrived.

Not everything needs to be explained.

Some things are simply felt, remembered, and carried forward.
